Amitabh Bachhan

Amitabh Bachchan is an Indian actor, playback singer, and producer who primarily works in Hindi cinema. He is widely considered one of the greatest, most accomplished, and commercially successful actors in the history of Indian cinema. His dominance in the Indian film industry during the 1970s–80s led the French director François Truffaut to describe him as a "one-man industry." He is the holder of numerous records, including the most nominations in any major acting category at Filmfare, with 42 nominations overall, as listed by Wikipedia's List of awards and nominations received by Amitabh Bachchan.

Bachchan has received several awards from the government of India: the Padma Shri (1984), the Padma Bhushan (2001), and the Padma Vibhushan (2015), detailed by Jagran Josh. In 2019, the Government of India awarded him the Dadasaheb Phalke Award, India's highest award in the field of cinema for the year 2018. Having debuted in 1969, he has appeared in over 200 Indian films in a career spanning more than five decades, according to his Wikipedia biography. Bachchan has won a record four National Film Awards in the Best Actor category.

Career

Early career

Bachchan made his film debut in 1969 as a voice narrator in Mrinal Sen's National Award-winning film Bhuvan Shome. His first acting role was as one of the seven protagonists in the film Saat Hindustani. The Indian audience began to recognize him with Anand (1971), and he first gained massive popularity in the early 1970s with films such as Zanjeer, Deewaar, and Sholay, where he was dubbed India's first "angry young man". In 1982, Bachchan was gravely injured while shooting a fight scene against Puneet Issar for the movie Coolie in Bangalore, requiring an emergency splenectomy, as recalled by dontcallitbollywood. Performance dip

Late 1990s: Dry spell

By the late 1990s, Bachchan was struggling with his career after a number of flop movies and the collapse of his maiden production venture, Amitabh Bachchan Corporation Limited (ABCL), as explained by Dailyo. He was under huge debt and on the verge of bankruptcy, sitting with four back-to-back box office failures which released in 1999—Lal Badshah, Sooryavansham, Hindustan Ki Kasam, and Kohram. During this time, the attempts to show a changed Amitabh Bachchan from an angry young man to an aging figure failed to impress audiences.

2000–present: Comeback and later years

In 2000, the television show Kaun Banega Crorepati (KBC) infused a new lease of life into his career. Though hesitant initially to switch from the big screen to television, his financial compulsions led him to host the show, which became a massive success and helped him recover financially. He returned to massive critical success in films, winning the National Film Award for Best Actor for his subsequent performances in Black (2006), Paa (2010), and Piku (2016). He has since been honored with numerous international recognitions, including being crowned Superstar of the Millennium at the 2000 Filmfare Awards and receiving the Officer of the Legion of Honour by the Government of France in 2007.
